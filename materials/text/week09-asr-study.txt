## Slide 5

Read the signal before choosing a method

                         Time →; frequency ↑; darker = stronger energy. A phoneme is a sound category that can distinguish words. Source: Jurafsky & Martin, Fig. 14.22.
5
                         
Notes:

Purpose and visual walkthrough

Use the supplied spectrogram as a brief phonetics anchor. Read left to right through “she just had a baby”; point out that the word labels are annotations over a continuous signal, not gaps automatically supplied to a recognizer.

Origins of values

The figure is Jurafsky and Martin, supplied chapter 14, Figure 14.22, page 19, August 2025 draft. Its time and phonetic labels come from the source example. The plot is a source illustration, not a new experiment. The English example is retained for teaching a source figure; project recordings must follow the separate non-English rule.

Step-by-step reasoning

A spectrogram displays changing frequency content over time; darker regions represent stronger energy in this source. The word and sound labels were aligned to that signal. A phoneme is a language-specific sound category that can distinguish words. Realizations vary with speaker and neighboring sounds, so a recognizer cannot treat every pause as a word separator or every sound category as one fixed waveform.

Teaching script and check question

Spend no more than 2.5 minutes here. Ask: does every word boundary require silence? Answer: no. The practical consequence is to learn temporal representations and alignments. Do not expand into anatomy, IPA memorization, or a phoneme inventory. The later front-end section explains how time-frequency features are computed.

## Slide 6

Why fixed sound templates are fragile

Linguistic category
A phoneme can distinguish words within a language. It is not one universal waveform.

Recorded signal
Speaker, speaking rate, surrounding sounds, microphone, and noise alter the measurements.
Coarticulation means neighboring speech sounds influence one another.

Evaluate on new speakers and conditions; matching training recordings is insufficient.
6
                         
Notes:

Purpose and visual walkthrough

Connect the short phonetics anchor to the experimental design. The two columns distinguish a linguistic abstraction from the recorded measurements supplied to a recognizer.

Origins of values

No acoustic values or universal phoneme counts are assumed. The listed sources of variability are qualitative and grounded in the supplied acoustic-phonetics material.

Step-by-step reasoning

Different recordings can represent the same linguistic category. Conversely, similar sound measurements can occur in different contexts. Coarticulation explains why neighboring sounds affect a realization. The model therefore needs contextual evidence, and its evaluation needs recordings beyond those used to train or tune it.

Teaching script and check question

Ask whether a model that recognizes one speaker’s training recordings has demonstrated generalization. Answer: no. It may have learned that speaker or recording setup; held-out speakers and conditions reveal the limitation. Keep phonetics tied to data and alignment rather than memorization.

## Slide 13

WER can exceed 100%

Supplied reference: “go”. Prediction: “please go now today”.

C = 1, S = 0, D = 0, I = 3, N = 1

WER = (0 + 0 + 3)/1 = 3 = 300%
C counts matching words; N counts reference words.
The three extra words are insertions.
There is no fixed upper bound on insertions.

WER is an error ratio. Word accuracy defined as 1 − WER can become negative.
13
                         
Notes:

Purpose and visual walkthrough

The four predicted words and one reference word are invented explanatory text. Three unmatched words are counted as insertions; the shared go is a match.

## Slide 26

Match error rate and word information lost

                         C: correct words; S: substitutions; D: deletions; I: insertions. N: reference words; M: predicted words. MER and WIL remain lexical measures.
26
                         
Notes:

Purpose and visual walkthrough

Use one alignment to compare word error rate, match error rate and word information lost. The denominators are shown from the correct/error counts rather than introduced as unexplained constants.

Origins of values

The supplied reference is go, while the prediction is please go now today. C=1 correct word, S=0 substitutions, D=0 deletions and I=3 insertions. N counts reference words and M counts predicted words.

Step-by-step reasoning

N=C+S+D=1 and M=C+S+I=4. WER=(S+D+I)/N=3. MER=(S+D+I)/(C+S+D+I)=3/4. WIL=1−(C/N)(C/M)=1−(1/1)(1/4)=3/4. Displayed percentages are 300%,75%,75%. These are lexical alignment measures; WIL is not verified semantic understanding. Definitions: https://github.com/jitsi/jiwer
