## Slide 16

From frequency bins to one log-mel feature

                         FFT (fast Fourier transform) exposes frequency bins; square magnitudes to obtain power. One mel-band energy = 0.25×2 + 1×4 + 0.25×2 = 5; log₁₀(5) ≈ 0.699. MFCC = mel-frequency cepstral coefficients: cosine-transform log-mel values.
16
                         
Notes:

Purpose and visual walkthrough

Turn a mel filter into a reproducible weighted-band calculation. The three panels distinguish raw bin power, weights and weighted contribution. This demonstrates one filter rather than claiming a full filterbank consists of three bins.

Origins of values

Powers[2,4,2] and filter weights[0.25,1,0.25] are supplied teaching values. Their units are arbitrary power units. The triangular weights express one example band. The logarithm shown is base10; actual checkpoint preprocessors may use natural logs or decibels.

Step-by-step reasoning

Multiply each power by its corresponding weight:2×0.25=0.5,4×1=4,2×0.25=0.5. Sum to get one band energy5. Log10(5)=0.69897≈0.699. A complete mel bank repeats this for each band and time frame. MFCC applies a discrete cosine transform to the log-mel vector, commonly retaining fewer coefficients.

Teaching script and check question

Ask students to name the origin of5: it is a weighted sum, not a probability. Ask whether the log base can be changed in a pretrained model without consequence: no, its processor defines its expected scaling. Use its processor for model inference.

## Slide 19

Viterbi: choose the strongest incoming path

                         A/B are example hidden states. Arrow labels = transition probabilities; right multipliers = frame-2 likelihoods. A: 0.1×max(0.8×0.7,0×0) = 0.056. B: 0.9×max(0.8×0.3,0×1) = 0.216. max selects the larger incoming score. Backtrack from B: the best path is A→B.
19
                         
Notes:

Purpose and visual walkthrough

Follow the time-indexed state graph and choose the strongest incoming path at each node. This is Viterbi dynamic programming on two frames, with a backpointer selecting the winning path.

Origins of values

A/B are illustrative hidden states. Initial probabilities are A1/B0. Frame1 emission likelihood densities are A0.8/B0.2; frame2 densities are A0.1/B0.9. Arrow labels are transition probabilities A→A0.7,A→B0.3,B→A0,B→B1. The emissions are continuous-observation density values, not a probability distribution across the two observed frames.

Step-by-step reasoning

First scores: A=1×0.8=0.8, B=0×0.2=0. At frame2, score(A)=0.1×max(0.8×0.7,0×0)=0.1×0.56=0.056. score(B)=0.9×max(0.8×0.3,0×1)=0.9×0.24=0.216. max chooses the stronger incoming route. Backtracking from the best final state B yields A→B. These are path scores, not normalized confidence.

Teaching script and check question

Have students compare0.56 versus0 at the upper node, then0.24 versus0 at the lower. Only after multiplying by the frame2 likelihood compare final states. Explain that real long sequences use log scores and retained predecessor pointers.

## Slide 20

Slide a learned pattern across audio frames

                         Supplied input/kernel; no padding or bias. Stride 2 advances the window by two positions. The same kernel multiplies each three-value window; sum its products to get one output. Output length = floor((5−3)/2)+1 = 2. Neural libraries use this unflipped-kernel convention.

Supplied first-token probabilities are P(A)=0.6 and P(B)=0.4. After A the next-token probabilities are[0.4,0.3,0.3]; after B they are[0.9,0.05,0.05]. These are chosen instructional distributions and each sums to1. Paths have equal length, with no length penalty or external language model.

## Slide 23

HuBERT: create targets from untranscribed audio

                         Self-supervised learning (SSL) uses audio-derived targets before ASR fine-tuning on transcripts. k-means assigns each frame to its nearest center, then replaces each center with its group’s mean. HuBERT predicts masked cluster IDs. wav2vec 2.0 learns to distinguish a target audio representation from distractors.

Notes:

Purpose and visual walkthrough

Motivate self-supervision by abundant untranscribed audio and scarce labeled transcripts. Distinguish learning representations from producing a recognizer ready to run.

Origins of values

HuBERT and wav2vec2 are pretrained audio encoder families. HuBERT predicts cluster targets obtained from audio; wav2vec2 uses a contrastive objective against quantized latent targets. These targets are learned acoustic categories, not guaranteed human phonemes. Source Ch15 explains HuBERT. The point plot adds supplied scalar frame values[1,2,8,9], initial centers[1,8] and cluster IDs[0,0,1,1]. These are illustrative, not measured audio features.

Step-by-step reasoning

For HuBERT, compute acoustic features, assign each frame a cluster label, mask some encoder-input frames, and train prediction of their assigned labels. Recluster improved hidden representations in later stages. Then attach a text-token output layer and fine-tune on paired audio/transcripts, commonly with CTC. A base pretrained checkpoint without this ASR head cannot directly transcribe. Assign each point to its nearest center. New centers are(1+2)/2=1.5 and(8+9)/2=8.5. Cluster IDs are arbitrary labels; they do not name words.

Teaching script and check question

## Slide 25

CTC: add every path that gives the same text

                         ∅ = blank (no output). X is fixed audio; a is the one-token target. The frame distributions are supplied. CTC multiplies frame-label probabilities along a path, conditioned on X. P(a | X) = 0.18+0.42+0.12 = 0.72. The remaining empty path has probability 0.28.

Notes:

Purpose and visual walkthrough

Explain the difference between the most probable path and the most probable transcript. This is the central reason CTC sums over alignments during training and prefix decoding.

Origins of values

There are two time steps with label alphabet{a,blank}. Supplied per-frame distributions are[0.6,0.4] at frame1 and[0.3,0.7] at frame2. Each sums to1. The acoustic encoder already uses context; CTC factorizes output labels conditioned on that encoded input.

Step-by-step reasoning

## Slide 27

RNN-T: track audio time and emitted tokens

                         RNN-T = recurrent neural network transducer. t counts audio steps; u counts emitted tokens. The joint network combines audio state h(t) with token-history state g(u), then scores token or blank. A token increases u; blank increases t. Streaming also requires limited look-ahead and buffering.
27
                         
Notes:

Purpose and visual walkthrough

Motivate a transducer by conditioning on previous emitted tokens while retaining an alignment mechanism over streaming audio. Distinguish model architecture from an implementation that merely chunks offline audio.

Origins of values

RNN-T stands for recurrent neural network transducer; modern transducers may use nonrecurrent encoders. The source shows an acoustic encoder, prediction network for token history, and joint network. No universal chunk length or latency is assumed.

Step-by-step reasoning

At position(t,u), acoustic state h(t) summarizes permitted audio context and prediction state g(u) summarizes the u already emitted tokens. The joint network maps both to a distribution over next tokens plus blank. Emitting a token advances u; emitting blank advances the audio time index t. Scores sum over possible alignments in training. Streaming requires a causal or limited-lookahead encoder and measured buffering, not just an RNN-T label.
